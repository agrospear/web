Emulsifiable Concentrate (EC) is one of the longest-established and most widely used agrochemical formulation types. The active ingredient is dissolved in an organic solvent together with emulsifier blends, producing a clear or translucent liquid that forms a stable oil-in-water emulsion when diluted in the spray tank.

## How EC formulation works

The active ingredient is fully dissolved in a solvent system — typically an aromatic or aliphatic hydrocarbon, or a polar solvent such as N-methylpyrrolidone depending on solubility. A blend of non-ionic and anionic emulsifiers is added at a ratio optimized for the specific solvent-ai pair. When the farmer pours the EC into water, the emulsifier system creates a fine oil-in-water emulsion with droplets typically in the 1–5 μm range, distributing the active ingredient uniformly throughout the spray volume.

## Benefits of EC formulations

EC formulations remain a staple of crop protection for several practical reasons:

- **Fast action** — the solvent carrier can penetrate cuticular wax layers, accelerating uptake and rainfastness
- **Excellent penetration** — dissolved active ingredient enters plant tissue more readily than suspended particles, giving superior contact and translaminar activity
- **Simple production** — dissolution and blending require less specialized equipment than wet milling (SC) or granulation (WDG), making EC cost-effective at scale
- **Broad compatibility** — EC tank-mixes well with many other formulation types and adjuvants
- **High active loading** — many active ingredients reach higher concentrations in EC than in water-based formulations
