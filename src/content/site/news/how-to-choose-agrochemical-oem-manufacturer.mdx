### What "Reliable" Means for an Agrochemical OEM Factory

A reliable OEM manufacturer is one that can be verified — you can check its production floor, its quality records and its certification documents — and one that behaves predictably: clear specifications, disciplined sampling, written commitments and no shortcuts on confidentiality. Everything below is what we would check if we were in your position.

### 1. Verify It Is Actually a Factory

The single biggest risk in agrochemical sourcing is not a bad factory — it is a trading company presenting itself as one. Red flags include: no floor photos or equipment lists, no willingness to schedule a video walkthrough, "minimum order quantities" that make no sense for a production plant, and catalogs that never name a specific production line.

What a real factory can show you: a named manufacturing plant (with an address you can look up), equipment like SC/EC production lines, WP/WDG granulation equipment, HPLC and GC instruments in the QC lab, a production capacity, and a floor you can tour on video before committing. We run a plant in Qingdao with dedicated SC, EC, WP/WDG and FS production lines — that kind of statement should be verifiable, not decorative.

### 2. Assess the Depth of Quality Control

Anyone can claim "strict QC." What matters is where the checks sit in the process:

- **Incoming material QC** — active ingredient purity, solvent quality and adjuvant specifications checked before they enter the line.
- **In-process checkpoints** — milling particle size, emulsion stability and suspension rate verified during production, not after.
- **Stability testing** — accelerated and long-term stability data generated for every formulation, stored under ICH conditions.
- **Final inspection** — content analysis, pH, viscosity, suspension rate and packaging checks against the approved sample.
- **Batch traceability** — each batch tied to a production record and a Certificate of Analysis (COA) you can request.

Ask for a sample COA and a QC record template. A real factory has both; a trader does not.

### 3. Verify Certifications Are Real

ICAMA license, ISO 9001 and FAO/WHO specification compliance are table stakes for an export-focused agrochemical factory, but certificates are also the easiest documents to counterfeit. Verify three things: the license/certificate number, the issuing authority, and the scope of the certificate (it must cover pesticide manufacturing, not just "trading").

Also ask when the last factory audit happened and whether you may see the audit summary. In most import markets, your registration dossier links back to the manufacturer's production documentation — a gap here becomes your problem at border control.

### 4. Judge the Factory by Its Sample Behavior

How a factory handles sampling tells you more than its website. Watch for:

- Does it push back with R&D questions (active ingredient compatibility, target concentration, adjuvant system, target pest spectrum) instead of just saying yes?
- Does sampling take 7–15 days as promised, with a documented sample-approval sheet and COA?
- Does the production sample match the pre-production sample?

A factory that treats your sample as a contract — documented, analyzed, approved — is a factory you can scale with.

### 5. Check How It Handles Intellectual Property

Your formulations, label designs and registration data are the most valuable thing you will hand over. A reliable partner signs an NDA before any file exchange and states in writing that your artwork, specifications and registration files remain your property and are never reused, resold or shown to another client. Walk away from any factory that treats confidentiality as negotiable.

### 6. Ask These 10 Questions

1. Can we verify your factory address, floor photos and equipment list?
2. How many tons of agrochemical formulations do you produce annually, and who audits your QC?
3. Can you share a blank COA and an HPLC analysis template?
4. What is your lab sample lead time, and what does a sample cost?
5. What is your production lead time after PO and deposit?
6. Which certifications and ICAMA licenses do you hold, with certificate numbers?
7. Do you sign an NDA before receiving formulation or label files?
8. Are the active ingredient content and physical properties tested and documented per batch?
9. What trade terms do you support (FOB, CIF, DDP)?
10. Can we schedule an on-site or video audit during production?

### Red Flags Summary

No production address, no QC paperwork, certificates you cannot verify, sample timelines that slip without explanation, and pressure to pay a large deposit before seeing a sample — any one of these is enough to keep looking. The right factory earns your order by being boringly verifiable, step by step.

### FAQ

**What is the difference between an OEM factory and a trading company?** A factory owns the production floor — it can show you the plant, the equipment and the QC process, and it answers directly for quality. A trading company resells other factories' output and adds a layer of margin, delay and responsibility transfer. Verify the entity you are contracting with (legal name, address, photos) before exchanging files.

**How many tons should I order for the first trial?** Pilot batches start at 5 tons on standard formulations; sample batches run 1–4 tons. That volume is enough to validate formulation quality, label design and packaging on real production output without committing to a full batch — the standard volume tier is 20+ tons.

**Can I audit the factory during production?** Yes — reputable OEM partners support third-party inspection (DUPRO during production, PSI before shipment) and on-site or video audits. Ask about both options in writing before you place the PO.

**Do you sign an NDA before receiving design files?** Yes. We sign an NDA before reviewing non-public formulations, label designs or registration plans, and your files stay confidential project material.
