### The Agrochemical Distribution Opportunity

The global crop protection market exceeds $65 billion, and regional distributors are the primary channel through which most farmers access agrochemical products. For agricultural trading companies, cooperatives, and entrepreneurs, building an agrochemical distribution brand requires moderate capital compared to manufacturing — with strong recurring revenue from seasonal demand.

### Registration Strategy

Registration is the gating factor in agrochemical distribution. Planning must start 12–24 months before first commercial sales:

- **Market selection**: Confirm which AI and formulations are registered or registerable in your target market. Some countries restrict certain AI classes.
- **Registration dossier**: Five-batch analysis, product specification, acute toxicity data, and efficacy data — your OEM manufacturer should provide the technical dossier.
- **Timeline**: Registration takes 6–18 months depending on the country. Factor this into your business plan.
- **Cost**: $2,000–$15,000 per product per country — budget accordingly.
- **Parallel strategy**: File registration for 5–8 products simultaneously to launch a complete portfolio, not a single product.

### Seasonal Cash Flow Planning

Agrochemical distribution is fundamentally seasonal. Cash flow management is critical:

- **Pre-season (3–6 months before planting)**: Registration filing, inventory procurement, sales team training, farmer engagement events
- **In-season (planting through harvest)**: Peak sales, delivery, technical support, field monitoring
- **Post-season**: Payment collection, inventory assessment, returns and claims processing, planning for next season

**Working capital recommendation**: Maintain enough capital to finance 50–70% of annual inventory before the season starts. For a mid-size distributor, this means $80,000–$200,000 in working capital. Farmer credit terms of 30–90 days after delivery are common — plan for delayed cash collection.

### Inventory Management Best Practices

- **Demand forecasting**: Use historical sales data and planting area estimates. Over-order herbicides (higher demand certainty) and under-order niche fungicides (lower predictability).
- **Shelf life management**: Most agrochemical formulations have 2-year shelf life. Track expiration dates and plan clearance before the last effective season.
- **Safety stock**: Maintain 10–15% buffer on top-selling SKUs. Stock-outs during peak season destroy farmer trust.
- **Container-level ordering**: Order full containers (20–25 tons) to minimize per-ton freight cost. Split shipments across formulations within the same container.

### Technical Support for Farmers

The most successful distributors invest in technical capability:

- **Agronomist sales team**: Hire agricultural graduates who can prescribe treatment programs, not just sell product
- **Demo plots and field days**: Show farmers the product working in their own conditions — the most effective sales tool in agriculture
- **Spray calibration support**: Help farmers apply correctly — proper dosage and timing drives both efficacy and repeat purchase
- **Seasonal crop protection calendar**: Provide farmers a seasonal spray schedule tailored to local pest pressure — builds loyalty and planned demand

### FAQ

**How many products do I need to start?** Most successful distributors launch with 5–8 SKUs covering the primary pest problems of one or two key crops. A narrow, well-supported portfolio builds farmer trust faster than a broad, thin range.

**Do I need registration before I can sell?** Yes — in virtually all markets, agrochemical products must be registered before commercial sale. Registration timelines of 6–18 months mean you should start the process 12–24 months before your target launch date. Your OEM manufacturer should provide the full technical dossier.

**How do I handle farmer credit?** Farmer credit is standard in most markets — 30–90 day terms after delivery are common. Build this into your working capital plan. Successful distributors limit credit to known, reliable accounts and use deposit requirements for new customers.

**Can I get a sample order to test before committing?** Yes — sample batches of 1–4 tons let you verify product quality, generate registration data, and conduct field trials before the main inventory order.
