### The Direct Answer

Private-label agrochemicals means selling crop protection products manufactured by a factory under your own brand — the factory formulates, you own the market. The process runs in six steps: **(1)** choose a factory you can verify, **(2)** send your specification, **(3)** approve samples, **(4)** place the PO at the right MOQ tier, **(5)** receive compliance documents, and **(6)** take delivery on your shipping terms. The whole journey — from first specification to a delivered container — typically takes 12–16 weeks: about 1 week for R&D review and quotation, 2–3 weeks for lab samples, 25–35 days of production, and 4–6 weeks of sea freight. This guide walks every step and links to the deep-dive article for each one.

### Step 1: Choose a Factory You Can Verify

The factory choice decides everything downstream — pricing, quality, compliance and delivery. Verify four things before you talk details: the plant (factory photos, capacity, in-house R&D lab), the quality system (QC checkpoints, HPLC/GC analysis, stability testing), the certifications (ICAMA license, ISO 9001, FAO/WHO specification compliance — certificates on request) and the company structure (who you are actually contracting with). A factory that is also a trading company, or that resells competitors' products, should be a red flag. Read the full checklist in [How to Choose a Reliable Agrochemical OEM Manufacturer](/news/how-to-choose-agrochemical-oem-manufacturer).

### Step 2: Send Your Specification

Eight items turn your idea into a quotable project: active ingredient and concentration, formulation type, target crop and pest, adjuvant preferences, label design and packaging specifications, volume and MOQ, target-market registration status, and budget with timeline. You do not need full registration dossiers — a reference product, a target specification or even a competitor's label is enough to start. With complete information, the factory returns an R&D review and quotation within one business day. Details in [What Information Does a Brand Need Before Starting Agrochemical Production](/news/info-needed-before-agrochemical-production).

### Step 3: Approve Samples

Expect the first lab sample in 7–14 days. Use the sample round to validate the formulation itself and the label on real packaging — screen colors do not survive bottle or bag printing without a Pantone reference or printed proof. Lock the formulation at this stage: changes after sample approval (different adjuvant system, new solvent, changed concentration) restart R&D and may re-trigger stability testing. The full sampling logic sits inside [How Long Does It Take To Develop A Custom Formulation](/news/custom-formulation-development-timeline).

### Step 4: Place the PO at the Right MOQ Tier

Your volume picks the tier: 1–4 tons for sample batches, 5 tons for a pilot run, 20+ tons for standard volume production, and specialty formulations with complex adjuvant systems run at the appropriate volume tier. Unit price per ton is driven by volume, active ingredient cost, formulation complexity, adjuvant system, packaging type and registration support scope — not by a price list. A pilot batch first is the safest entry: real production quality, real QC data, controlled investment. The complete pricing model is in [How Agrochemical OEM MOQ and Pricing Work](/news/agrochemical-oem-moq-pricing).

### Step 5: Receive Compliance Documents

ICAMA certificate, FAO/WHO specification compliance, five-batch analysis, stability data, toxicology summaries and local registration dossiers must travel with the goods. Verify that the certificate names your exact formulation, that reports back the certificate, and that the classification matches your target market — a certificate for "insecticide SC" that does not name your specific formulation is not coverage. Ask for the certificate number at quotation stage, not after production. How to check the paperwork properly: [ICAMA Registration for Agrochemicals — What Buyers Must Check](/news/icama-registration-agrochemical).

### Step 6: Take Delivery on Your Terms

Production completes 25–35 days after PO and deposit; sea freight adds 4–6 weeks to the EU, Africa or South America. Choose the incoterm that matches your experience: FOB Qingdao if you import regularly with your own forwarder, CIF to split freight and destination handling, DDP for one predictable total price delivered to your warehouse — the most popular choice for new brands. Shipping documents (packing list, invoice, bill of lading) travel ahead of the vessel. Compare the terms in [the private-label OEM guide](/news/private-label-agrochemical-oem-guide).

### Three Reference Launch Scenarios (Indicative Budgets)

Product cost depends on active ingredient, formulation type, adjuvant system, packaging and quantity — these illustrative scenarios give first-time brand owners a planning ballpark. All prices are **indicative, EXW Qingdao**, and the exact quotation is confirmed against your configuration (see [How Agrochemical OEM MOQ and Pricing Work](/news/agrochemical-oem-moq-pricing) for the cost drivers).

**Scenario A — Pilot batch (5 tons, single formulation, standard packaging).** Pilot production at a per-ton premium consistent with small-scale manufacturing. Use case: a local market test, registration support filing, or a single-crop program. Lowest capital entry beyond sample quantities.

**Scenario B — Small brand launch (20 tons, 2 formulations, custom label, branded packaging).** Production at improved per-ton pricing with branded bottles or bags and custom label design. Use case: regional distribution across 2–3 crops or pest targets.

**Scenario C — Scaled launch (100+ tons, 3–5 SKUs, optimized packaging, registration dossier support).** Volume production at the most competitive per-ton rate, with full registration documentation support and marketing-optimized packaging. Use case: national or multi-country distribution program.

Add logistics and your own budget on top: sea freight to the EU, Africa or South America runs roughly 4–6 weeks, plus duties and local delivery, and a 10–15% contingency buffer is the norm. First production reaches your warehouse about **8–12 weeks after PO (production), plus the freight leg** — plan 90–120 days ahead of the season for your first order.

### The 12–16 Week Timeline at a Glance

- **Week 1** — specification submitted, R&D review and quotation within one business day, PO signed.
- **Weeks 2–4** — lab sample production; sample approval ends the round.
- **Weeks 5–10** — mass production (25–35 days) with QC, HPLC/GC analysis and stability testing.
- **Weeks 11–14** — sea freight to destination with documents ahead of the vessel.
- **Week 15–16** — customs clearance and delivery to your warehouse.

Timeline, pricing, compliance and logistics are the four questions every new brand asks — the guides above answer each one in depth, and [Custom Formulation Development — From Concept to Container](/product-development) walks the R&D side of the same journey. When you are ready, the factory will quote your project within one business day.

### Common Mistakes First-Time Agrochemical Brand Founders Make

- **Launching too many SKUs at once** — five-plus formulations dilute focus, multiply inventory risk and complicate registration; start with 1–2.
- **Ignoring registration timelines in launch planning** — under-estimating the sample → pilot → volume chain plus registration filing and sea freight is how brands miss their seasonal window.
- **Choosing the cheapest formulation without testing it** — a low-grade product performs and fails differently in the field; a pilot batch protects your brand reputation for a controlled investment.
- **Underinvesting in field trials** — even a great formulation sells poorly without local efficacy data; budget proper field trials before the first commercial push.
- **No after-sales tech support plan** — without a clear phytotoxicity response and replacement process, an otherwise fine product becomes a support problem.
- **Leaving compliance thinking until late** — ICAMA certificates, FAO/WHO specifications and local registration dossiers must be specified at quotation stage, not discovered at customs ([ICAMA Registration for Agrochemicals](/news/icama-registration-agrochemical)).

### FAQ

**Do I need my own formulations for private label?** No — you can start from a proven platform with your brand, label and packaging (ODM route), or develop your own formulation (OEM route). Both end with your brand on the product.

**How much capital do I need to start?** The entry point is the pilot tier at 5 tons; the first real order is typically the 20-ton standard batch. Production starts against a deposit, so your cash exposure is staged: deposit → balance before shipment.

**Who handles customs?** Under DDP the factory does — duties and delivery included in one price. Under FOB or CIF, you or your customs broker handle destination-side costs and clearance.

**What if the sample does not match my expectations?** That is exactly what the sample round is for: you request revisions before production starts. Most sample corrections are label, packaging or adjuvant adjustments; formulation changes may add R&D time or re-testing.

**Can I sell in multiple markets with one production run?** Yes — but tell the factory all target markets before production so the document set covers ICAMA, FAO/WHO and local registration requirements in one shipment.
